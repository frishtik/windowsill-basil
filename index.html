Keeping Basil Alive on a North-Facing Apartment Windowsill

Basil wants six to eight hours of sun, and a north-facing window gives it maybe two of bright indirect light. It can still live there, but it needs help.

Add a light. A small full-spectrum LED grow light, 15–20 watts, about 20 centimetres above the leaves on a timer for 12 hours a day makes the difference between a leggy, pale plant and a bushy one.

Water from the bottom when the top two centimetres of soil are dry. In low light basil drinks slowly, and soggy soil rots the roots faster than anything else. Use a pot with a drainage hole and pour out what's left in the saucer after half an hour.

Keep it warm and away from the glass in winter. Basil sulks below 10°C, and a windowsill in January can be much colder than the room.

Pinch it often. Take off the top pair of leaves above a leaf node every week, and pick off flower buds the moment they show. A basil plant that flowers stops making leaves.

Expect smaller leaves than a summer garden plant, but a steady supply for cooking all winter.
